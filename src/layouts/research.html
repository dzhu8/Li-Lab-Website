Our Research

Tissues are ecosystems of cells that constantly communicate with one another. This communication must be precisely regulated in space and time to generate tissue form during development, maintain function during homeostasis, and enable adaptive responses to physiological stress. We seek to understand how and why cell–cell communication is spatially and temporally patterned, and how these patterns shape tissue organization and function.

Approaches

Biological patterns emerge from interactions spanning a wide range of spatial and temporal scales—from the assembly of nanometer-scale protein complexes at the cell surface to the formation of tissue structures hundreds of microns across, and from millisecond-scale molecular dynamics to the days-long processes of organ morphogenesis. To uncover the fundamental principles that bridge these scales and give rise to life’s intricate architecture, we develop integrated bottom-up and top-down approaches inspired by developmental biology, synthetic biology, biophysics, and computational biology. Ultimately, our goal is to build predictive, mechanistic models of cell–cell communication that can inform tissue engineering strategies and therapeutic intervention.
