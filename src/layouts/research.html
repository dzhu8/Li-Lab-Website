Our Research

The transition from single cells to multicellular organisms fundamentally expanded the ways biological systems process information, enabling tissues to generate behaviors that no individual cell could achieve alone—from building organs during development to adapting to changing physiological demands. Our lab uses experimentally tractable systems to investigate how different multicellular systems solve these information-processing problems and to uncover principles that can generalize across related biological contexts. Specifically, we ask three interconnected questions: how information propagates across tissues, how diverse cells collectively compute information to make tissue-level decisions, and whether these processes can be reconstructed, predicted, and engineered.
