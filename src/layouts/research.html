Our Research

The transition from single cells to multicellular organisms fundamentally expanded the ways biological systems process information. Individual cells encode information through the identity, strength, temporal dynamics, and combinations of signaling pathways. Multicellular organization creates entirely new information-processing capabilities: information can be propagated across space, distributed among specialized cell types, and transformed through interactions among neighboring cells. These collective properties allow tissues to generate behaviors that no individual cell could achieve alone, from building organs during development to adapting to changing physiological demands. Our laboratory seeks to uncover the principles of multicellular information processing through three interconnected research themes.
