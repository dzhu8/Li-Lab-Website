Multicellular Information Processing

Living tissues are nature’s most sophisticated information-processing systems. They continuously sense, propagate, and compute information to build organs, maintain physiological homeostasis, and adapt to changing environments. Our laboratory combines synthetic biology, quantitative biology, and computational modeling to uncover the principles of multicellular information processing—and to make living tissues predictable and ultimately programmable.

📄 Hedgehog diffusion (PDF)

Diffusion barriers imposed by tissue topology shape Hedgehog morphogen gradients

PNAS, July 15, 2024 (PubMed: 39190357)

Gavin Schlissel, Miram Meziane, Domenic Narducci, Anders S. Hansen, Pulin Li

Using single-molecule imaging, we determined that Sonic hedgehog transitioned between membrane-confined and unconfined states aided by SCUBE1, identifying a mechanism by which morphogen diffusion is controlled.
